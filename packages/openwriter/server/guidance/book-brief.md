## Attached book brief

This is an ordinary reviewable document that records the book's direction and points to its authoritative sources. The workspace attachment makes its role explicit; its title, a canonical tag or a generated logline alone does not make every statement approved. The empty brief from createBrief has seven sections: Purpose and reader promise, Audience, Voice and form, How we work, Author decisions, Sources and evidence, Open questions.

The brief fills in from what the writer says, at whatever pace they like; it is never a prerequisite for writing or editing. Useful things to learn, a question at a time when the writer wants to talk it through: what kind of book it is (its genre and form), who it is for and what it gives them, how the writer sounds and how long the book runs, what is already decided, what material exists (drafts, posts, talks, transcripts, research, notes), and what is still open. What the writer has not said stays an open question; an unfilled section is not permission to invent the writer's position.

How we work holds the writer's own description of how they work, in their words: who writes the prose (the agent ghostwriting, the two co-writing, or the writer alone with the agent editing or organising), how they plan (outline first, draft first, chapter by chapter, or from an existing manuscript), and how they like to review. Under the Custom workflow it is what the agent follows; under Guided it can hold anything the writer wants done differently. Which workflow applies is the book's setting, never this section, and later sessions read both.

Existing thesis, audience, voice, research, decision and question documents can stay where they are and be linked by docId. Each link should explain the source's role. Historical decisions, superseded outlines, candidate material and unresolved proposals remain distinguished from current decisions.

### The writer's voice

Drafts the agent writes can only sound like the writer once OpenWriter has learned the writer's voice; a writer who writes their own prose does not need it. The writer's own writing goes into documents: chapters, posts, articles, newsletters, transcripts of talks or interviews in their own words. The writer then right-clicks those documents, or a folder of them, and chooses Learn my voice from this; an agent cannot start it. Only text the writer wrote counts: OpenWriter's authorship tracking credits text an agent writes into a document to the agent, so the writer pastes or types their own material, or it comes from documents they wrote. A profile is built once at least 200 words of the writer's own text are in, and more writing gives a closer match; writing that matches how the book should sound (spoken transcripts for a conversational book, for example) helps most. The voice status line in each book delivery shows the state, and get_voice shows what drafts will be written against. The Voice and form section can link the strongest samples.

The brief is kept concise enough to orient a new agent. Detailed source material remains in linked documents. Changes to the brief use the same accept/reject review as manuscript edits. Only accepted text enters the automatically delivered brief; pending changes remain proposals. A changed brief or directly linked source refreshes the delivered context in the current session.
