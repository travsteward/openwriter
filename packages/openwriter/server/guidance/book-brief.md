## Attached book brief

This is an ordinary reviewable document that records the book's direction and points to its authoritative sources. The workspace attachment makes its role explicit; its title, a canonical tag or a generated logline alone does not make every statement approved. The empty brief from createBrief has six sections: Purpose and reader promise, Audience, Voice and form, Author decisions, Sources and evidence, Open questions.

The brief is filled by interviewing the author, one question at a time, and writing down what they say. The first question is what kind of book it is: fiction or nonfiction, and for nonfiction whether it makes one extended argument or gives the complete picture of a subject. The answer decides how chapters get planned. Then who the reader is and what the book promises them; how the author sounds and how long and in what form the book runs; what is already decided; what material exists (the author's writing, talks, transcripts, research, notes); and what is still open. Answers the author has not given stay as open questions; an unfilled section is not permission to invent the author's position.

Existing thesis, audience, voice, research, decision and question documents can stay where they are and be linked by docId. Each link should explain the source's role. Historical decisions, superseded outlines, candidate material and unresolved proposals remain distinguished from current decisions.

### The writer's voice

Drafts can only sound like the writer once OpenWriter has learned the writer's voice, so it is set up while the brief is being filled. The writer's own writing goes into documents: posts, articles, newsletters, transcripts of talks or interviews in their own words. The writer then right-clicks those documents, or a folder of them, and chooses Learn my voice from this; an agent cannot start it. Only text the writer wrote counts: OpenWriter's authorship tracking credits text that an agent writes into a document to the agent, so the writer pastes or types their own material, or it comes from documents they wrote. A profile is built once at least 200 words of the writer's own text are in, and more writing gives a closer match. The voice status line in each book delivery shows the state, and get_voice shows what drafts will be written against. The Voice and form section can link the strongest samples.

The brief is kept concise enough to orient a new agent. Detailed source material remains in linked documents. Changes to the brief use the same accept/reject review as manuscript edits. Only accepted text enters the automatically delivered brief; pending changes remain proposals. A changed brief or directly linked source refreshes the delivered context in the current session.
